Welcome

This handbook captures what current and former members have learned about running the garden well. If you're new, read this before your first shift. If you've been around a while, add to it whenever you learn something worth passing on.

New member? Your first step is a short induction walk with a returning member. Contact the Garden Lead to arrange this — see the People page for details.

Roles

The garden has a small number of named roles that carry specific responsibilities. All other members share general maintenance duties equally.

  • Garden Lead — overall coordination, external correspondence, key holder
  • Plot Coordinators — one per bed cluster; tracks planting schedules and watering rotas
  • Equipment Steward — maintains tool inventory and orders supplies
  • Records Keeper — maintains this wiki, season logs, and meeting notes
  • Alumni Liaison — keeps graduates connected and channels their knowledge back in

Plot care

Each plot has a Coordinator responsible for its seasonal plan, but all members share watering and weeding duties during shared workdays (Saturdays, 9–12).

Key rule: Never remove or harvest from a plot that isn't yours without checking with its Coordinator first — even if something looks ready.

Planting plans for the current season live in the Plot Records section. Add your notes there at end of season so next year's members have a record.

Tools & supplies

All shared tools are stored in the wooden shed (lock code shared on the members group chat). Sign tools out on the clipboard inside the shed door if you borrow anything overnight.

Consumables (compost, seeds, ties) are ordered through the Equipment Steward. Raise requests at least two weeks before you need them.

Communication

Day-to-day chat happens in the group chat. Official decisions and anything that needs a record go into meeting notes (linked from the Plot Records section). External emails use the shared garden address — only the Garden Lead and Alumni Liaison send from this address.

Seasonal calendar

  • February–March — seed ordering, bed preparation, new member inductions
  • April–May — main planting, compost top-up, first workdays
  • June–August — weekly maintenance, harvest sharing, skill-share sessions
  • September–October — late harvest, season wrap-up, plot records updated
  • November–January — winter prep, planning next season, alumni check-in

Joining & leaving

New members join through the university student employment scheme or by direct invitation from an existing member. When you leave, please update your plot records, return all equipment, and let the Alumni Liaison know if you'd like to stay connected as a graduate member.
